Between July 16, 1945 — Trinity, New Mexico — and September 3, 2017 — North
Korea's sixth and largest confirmed test — at least 2,056 nuclear devices were
detonated on this planet. The countries that built and used them: the United
States, the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom, France, China, India, Pakistan,
and North Korea. Each test is a data point here; in reality, each was an event
that contaminated soil, displaced communities, and poisoned downwinders for
generations.

This scroll-driven visualization moves through time. As you scroll, tests appear
in chronological order. The size of each mark encodes yield (in kilotons); the
color encodes the testing nation. The pace of the Cold War is impossible to miss:
the US and USSR between them account for 85% of all tests, with the frenetic
period 1958–1962 producing nearly 300 detonations in under five years — before
the Partial Test Ban Treaty drove most testing underground.

The dataset was hand-curated from Johnston's Archive, the most comprehensive
public compilation of nuclear test records, cross-referenced against SIPRI's
nuclear database. Yields for many Soviet tests remain classified estimates.
Several small tactical weapon tests have ambiguous dates; they are placed at
mid-year for their reported calendar year.

**Methodology:** Static dataset, last reviewed June 2026. Tests with no
confirmed yield are displayed at minimum size. "Unknown" medium defaults to
"underground" post-1963 per treaty expectations.

**Source:** [Johnston's Archive](https://www.johnstonsarchive.net/nuclear/tests/) — Public Domain
